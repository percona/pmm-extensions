MySQL and MongoDB restores can no longer be scheduled. Both restore apps stop offering scheduling, which hides every schedule control for them in the PMM Extensions UI and in the PMM Extensions UI embedded in PMM. The PMM Extensions periodic-task gateway now answers `400 Bad Request` to `POST /api/extensions/periodic-tasks/{task_name}/` and `PUT /api/extensions/periodic-tasks/{id}` when the schedule would run a task that no installed app offers scheduling for — restores included, and likewise a task whose owner matches no installed app or is the default `ANY` — so a restore schedule can no longer be created, edited, re-enabled or disabled through that gateway. Listing and deleting schedules are unchanged, and the periodic routes of the Tasks service itself (`/api/tasks/`) are not guarded. Existing schedules of installed apps that do not offer scheduling (today the two restore apps) are not deleted: each is switched off at the first PMM Extensions startup after the upgrade, not at install time, logged at warning level by name, and stays stored until deleted. A running Celery beat stops firing it on its next schedule check, without a restart; until that PMM Extensions startup it keeps firing. A restore app already removed from `EXTENSIONS.APPS` is not swept, so its schedules keep their state. If that startup sweep cannot reach the Tasks database — a deployment whose `extensions` migrations have run ahead of its `tasks` migrations, for instance — PMM Extensions starts normally and logs the failure instead of refusing to boot, and the sweep runs at the next startup.
